Abby is an invoicing app for freelancers in France. A subscription business, so the same problem as yours. Getting someone to pay once is hard. Getting them to stay is where the money is.

The goal this summer was simple. Get free users to pay, and get as many as possible onto the annual plan.

What worked

Two subject lines did most of the work. “Lock in your price before it goes back up.” And “€37.80 sitting idle on your subscription.” 21.3% and 17.8% of the people who opened them clicked. The campaign average was 3.99%.

Both do the same thing. They put an exact number on what the reader is about to lose. Nobody wants to leave money on the table.

What didn't

One email was opened by almost half the people who got it, 49.7%. Then barely anyone clicked, 1.9%. The subject line promised more than the email gave. A big open rate means nothing on its own, and I'd rather tell you than hide it.

What I won't pretend

This is a comparison with the month before, not a controlled test. Some of these people would have subscribed anyway. And most email tools are generous when they count which sales came from an email.

That's exactly why, with you, a small group of customers doesn't get the new emails from day one. So the number we look at is the real one.

Questions

Will it still sound like us?

Yes. I read everything you've written before, and I talk to your customers. If it doesn't sound like you, it doesn't go out.

What if it doesn't work?

You'll see it in the numbers, because we measure from day one. Then you only pay the fixed fee, and you can stop after the first months.

How is my share calculated?

On what the system brings in above the baseline we set at the start, compared with the group that doesn't get the new emails. So you only pay for what it really adds.

When will I see results?

The first sends go out in the first month. The real progress shows after two or three months, once the system has learned enough about your customers.
